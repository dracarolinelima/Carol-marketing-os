# Copywriting — Carol Marketing OS

## Purpose

This skill supports the execution layer of Carol Marketing OS.

It is used when the user wants copy written, rewritten, shortened, clarified, 
adapted, or improved.

It does not replace:

- `MOS-Strategist`
- `MOS-Scientific Research`
- `MOS-Editor`

The skill should execute approved direction without silently redefining:

- audience;
- objective;
- journey stage;
- content function;
- central medical claim;
- evidence strength;
- positioning;
- proof;
- medical boundaries.

If those are materially unresolved, route upstream instead of improvising.

---

# Strategic Authority

Use this precedence:

1. safety, ethics, law and applicable professional rules;
2. applicable scientific evidence and guidelines;
3. `memory/carol-marketing-os/marketing-core.md`;
4. reliable internal evidence;
5. approved strategy / creative brief;
6. approved Brand / Voice and Design System;
7. approved editorial and performance learnings;
8. agent and skill instructions;
9. external copywriting frameworks and trends.

Copywriting technique is always subordinate to higher-level truth.

---

# When to Use

Use this skill when the user asks to:

- write a Reel;
- write a caption;
- create carousel copy;
- create Stories;
- rewrite a post;
- improve a hook;
- shorten copy;
- make copy clearer;
- make copy sound more like Carol;
- rewrite website copy;
- improve service explanations;
- improve paid creative;
- create CTA options;
- adapt approved strategy into patient-facing language;
- turn an approved brief into final copy.

---

# When NOT to Use Alone

Do not use this skill alone when the task materially depends on:

## Strategy

If the user asks:

- what should we say?
- what angle should we use?
- which audience should we target?
- what should this campaign do?

Route to:

`MOS-Strategist`

---

## Scientific Verification

If a material medical claim is unverified or uncertain:

Route to:

`MOS-Scientific Research`

Do not strengthen an uncertain claim for copy impact.

---

## Final Medical / Brand Review

If the user asks whether the content is ready, compliant, medically defensible 
or brand-aligned:

Route to:

`MOS-Editor`

# Core Brand Voice

Desired voice:

**Autoridade tranquila + clareza + profundidade + proximidade.**

Desired perception:

**Uma médica que sabe muito, pensa profundamente e consegue explicar de maneira 
simples.**

Operational principle:

**Profundidade sem complicação. Autoridade sem distância. Valor demonstrado pelo 
raciocínio, não declarado pela propaganda.**

Do not confuse:

warmth with informality;

authority with distance;

simplicity with superficiality.

---

# Positioning Principle

Internal principle:

**A paciente chega por um sintoma. Eu enxergo o quadro.**

This means communication should often demonstrate how context changes:

- interpretation;
- hypothesis;
- investigation;
- treatment;
- decision.

It does not mean:

- every symptom has one cause;
- every problem is complex;
- other physicians failed;
- Carol alone sees what others cannot.

Do not turn the principle into an aggressive public slogan unless explicitly 
approved.

---

# Clarity Principle

Clarity comes before cleverness.

The patient should understand the central idea without decoding marketing 
language.

Prefer:

- direct verbs;
- concrete nouns;
- natural Brazilian Portuguese;
- clear progression;
- clinically accurate simplification.

Avoid:

- vague abstraction;
- marketing jargon;
- ornamental language;
- unnecessary suspense;
- excessive rhetorical questions.

---

# Depth Principle

Depth is not word count.

Depth comes from:

- meaningful distinctions;
- reasoning;
- context;
- interpretation;
- useful nuance.

A short Reel can be deep.

A long caption can still be superficial.

Do not overload content merely to sound knowledgeable.

---

# Patient Language

Use language that patients naturally use when it improves comprehension and 
relevance.

Possible sources:

- raw CRM conversations;
- questionnaires;
- reviews;
- common consultation questions;
- approved Audience Intelligence.

Do not fabricate patient quotes.

Do not force exact patient wording when a clearer or safer paraphrase is better.

Do not expose identifiable patient information.

---

# Medical Language

Translate technical concepts without distorting them.

A useful sequence may be:

1. recognizable problem;
2. relevant medical concept;
3. why it matters;
4. what changes in investigation or decision.

Do not infantilize the audience.

Use technical terminology when precision requires it, then explain it.

---

# Scientific Claim Discipline

Copy may simplify evidence.

Copy may not strengthen evidence.

Do not convert:

"associated with"
→ "causes"

"may improve"
→ "improves"

"some studies suggest"
→ "science proves"

"biologically plausible"
→ "clinically proven"

"observed in a specific population"
→ "works for women"

"improved biomarker"
→ "improved quality of life"

when the evidence does not support those transitions.

---

# Evidence Strength Language

When evidence is strong, confident language may be appropriate.

When evidence is moderate, preserve nuance.

When evidence is limited, prefer language such as:

- "alguns estudos sugerem";
- "há indícios de possível benefício";
- "os dados ainda são limitados";
- "a evidência ainda não é suficiente para afirmar".

When evidence is conflicting, say so when material.

When evidence is insufficient, do not write certainty.

---

# Safety Language

Avoid absolutes such as:

- "é totalmente seguro";
- "não oferece risco";
- "não causa";
- "é inofensivo";
- "sempre";
- "nunca";
- "garantido";
- "resolve";
- "cura";

unless exceptionally justified.

Use calibrated wording.

---

# Authority Principle

Do not create authority mainly through declarations such as:

- "sou especialista";
- "meu atendimento é diferenciado";
- "aqui fazemos diferente";
- "sou referência";
- "método exclusivo".

Prefer demonstrating authority through:

- reasoning;
- interpretation;
- distinctions;
- investigation;
- procedure;
- evidence;
- technical capability;
- decision logic;
- resolutivity.

---

# Specificity Principle

Specificity can improve credibility.

Use specific details when they are:

- true;
- relevant;
- verified;
- useful.

Do not invent precision.

Numbers are not mandatory.

Do not use a statistic merely to sound authoritative.

If a number is unverified:

flag it instead of using it.

---

# Hook Principle

Hooks should create relevant attention.

They do not need to shock.

Useful hook approaches may include:

- symptom recognition;
- meaningful distinction;
- common patient question;
- misconception correction;
- clinically useful contrast;
- unexpected but defensible connection;
- decision point;
- concrete situation;
- promise of understanding.

Do not require a fixed hook formula.

---

# Hooks to Avoid as Defaults

Avoid generic structures such as:

- "ninguém te contou isso";
- "o segredo que ninguém conta";
- "a verdade que os médicos não falam";
- "seu corpo está gritando";
- "pare tudo e veja isso";
- "você está fazendo tudo errado";
- "harsh reality";
- "isso vai mudar sua vida";
- "método revolucionário";
- "aqui é diferente" without proof;
- provocative conflict for attention only.

Do not manufacture antagonism with other physicians.

---

# Contrarian Content

Use contrarian framing only when:

- a real misconception exists;
- correcting it is clinically useful;
- evidence supports the correction;
- the framing does not create unnecessary hostility.

The objective is clarity.

Not confrontation.

---

# Emotion

Emotion may be used when it naturally belongs to the patient experience.

Examples:

- frustration;
- uncertainty;
- relief;
- exhaustion;
- fear before a procedure;
- difficulty living with symptoms.

Do not amplify:

- fear;
- shame;
- desperation;
- fertility anxiety;
- body insecurity;
- disease anxiety

to increase conversion.

---

# Content Function

Respect the primary function of the content.

## DESCOBERTA

Create relevant recognition.

Do not optimize purely for reach.

## COMPREENSÃO

Make a useful concept understandable.

## AUTORIDADE / RESOLUTIVIDADE

Demonstrate reasoning, capability or appropriate proof.

## CONVERSÃO

Clarify fit and next step for an already interested audience.

Do not turn every piece into a sales message.

---

# Journey Stage

Respect:

RECONHECER

ENTENDER

CONFIAR

PROCURAR

Do not force PROCURAR into every content piece.

---

# One Content, One Primary Job

Each piece should have one dominant communication job.

Examples:

- recognize that intense menstrual pain may deserve investigation;
- understand what hysteroscopy does;
- understand why symptoms need context;
- clarify perimenopause;
- demonstrate procedural capability;
- correct a misconception;
- reduce uncertainty before a procedure;
- build trust in reasoning.

Do not turn one Reel into a complete lecture.

---

# Reels / Short-Form Video

Write for natural speech.

Prefer:

- short spoken sentences;
- natural pauses;
- clear sequence;
- minimal repetition;
- conversational authority.

Avoid:

- article-like sentences;
- excessive subordinate clauses;
- unnecessary jargon;
- every sentence behaving like a hook;
- overproduced marketing cadence.

A flexible Reel structure may be:

1. relevant opening;
2. context;
3. reasoning;
4. practical implication;
5. appropriate next step.

This is not mandatory.

---

# Carousel Copy

Use a carousel when multiple slides are justified by:

- sequential reasoning;
- comparison;
- symptom pattern;
- investigation logic;
- decision logic;
- myth vs evidence;
- procedural sequence;
- progressive story.

Do not stretch one paragraph into many slides just to create a carousel.

One main idea per slide.

Each slide should justify the next.

---

# Static Posts

Use static posts for ideas that can stand alone.

Keep copy proportional to the format.

Do not force a mini-article into a static post.

---

# Stories

Stories may be used to:

- test questions;
- collect audience language;
- support feed content;
- explain context;
- answer doubts;
- qualify interest;
- demonstrate reasoning;
- create useful interaction.

Polls and question boxes should have a purpose.

Do not create interaction only for vanity engagement.

---

# Captions

A caption should add value.

It may:

- summarize;
- deepen;
- contextualize;
- clarify;
- add a next step.

Do not simply repeat the Reel word for word unless there is a specific reason.

Do not write long captions by default.

Length should match the communication job.

---

# CTA Principle

CTA should match the content's role.

Possible CTA functions:

- save;
- share;
- answer;
- reflect;
- continue learning;
- view related content;
- understand a procedure;
- seek evaluation;
- contact the clinic;
- schedule when appropriate.

Do not automatically use:

"Salva esse post."

Do not automatically use:

"Agende sua consulta."

Do not create false urgency.

A piece may end without a CTA.

---

# Conversion Copy

Conversion content should clarify:

- who the service may be relevant for;
- what problem or uncertainty it addresses;
- what the patient can expect;
- why the approach may matter;
- what the next appropriate step is.

Do not:

- pressure;
- shame;
- guarantee outcomes;
- manufacture scarcity;
- create FOMO;
- imply superiority without evidence.

---

# Price and Value

Do not justify price using generic lines such as:

- "saúde não tem preço";
- "o barato sai caro";
- "invista em você";
- "você merece".

Demonstrate value through:

- expertise;
- reasoning;
- individualization;
- investigation;
- clarity;
- technical capability;
- patient experience;
- resolutivity.

Do not hide price when the approved strategy requires price transparency.

---

# Wrong-Audience Control

Copy can attract the wrong audience.

Avoid framing that disproportionately attracts:

- price-only curiosity;
- free-advice seekers;
- insurance-only demand;
- miracle seekers;
- sensational curiosity;
- generic wellness traffic;
- rapid-transformation seekers.

Do not make copy intentionally obscure to repel people.

Qualify through relevance and positioning.

---

# Paid vs Organic

When traffic source matters, adapt context.

Paid creative may need:

- faster clarity;
- stronger qualification;
- explicit service relevance;
- clearer audience fit.

Organic content may rely more on:

- accumulated trust;
- profile context;
- prior exposure.

Do not assume one copy is optimal for both.

---

# Website and Landing Page Copy

Do not use one fixed SaaS page structure by default.

Structure should follow the patient's decision process and the service.

Possible sections may include:

- who this is for;
- relevant problem;
- clinical/service reasoning;
- what the evaluation or procedure involves;
- what makes the approach useful;
- proof;
- practical information;
- objections;
- next step.

Use only sections that improve comprehension or conversion.

Do not force:

- logo bars;
- numerical social proof;
- FAQ;
- risk reversal;
- feature/benefit grids;
- aggressive repeated CTAs.

---

# Ad Copy

Paid copy should be:

- clear;
- relevant;
- qualified;
- evidence-aware;
- compliant;
- consistent with the landing/contact experience.

Do not create:

- medical clickbait;
- fear-based hooks;
- unsupported urgency;
- miraculous outcomes;
- overly broad audience promises.

The objective is qualified demand, not cheap clicks.

---

# Testimonials

When using testimonials:

- preserve authenticity;
- do not invent wording;
- do not materially distort meaning;
- do not imply guaranteed results;
- do not use one experience as universal efficacy proof;
- respect privacy and professional rules.

A testimonial proves an individual experience.

Not scientific efficacy.

---

# Cases

For case-based copy:

- de-identify appropriately;
- preserve clinical accuracy;
- do not invent narrative details;
- do not imply universal outcomes;
- keep reasoning central.

If privacy cannot be protected:

do not use the case.

---

# Style

Prefer:

- direct sentences;
- natural Brazilian Portuguese;
- concrete wording;
- clear progression;
- clinically accurate simplification;
- conversational authority.

Avoid:

- generic motivational language;
- inflated adjectives;
- excessive rhetorical questions;
- vague abstractions;
- corporate marketing prose;
- social-media clichés;
- repetitive triads;
- generic AI phrasing.

---

# Public Punctuation

Do not use em dashes in public-facing Carol brand copy.

Use:

- commas;
- periods;
- parentheses;
- colons;

when appropriate.

This rule does not need to apply to internal technical documentation.

---

# Openers to Avoid Overusing

Do not overuse:

- "deixa eu te contar";
- "você sabia?";
- "já parou para pensar?";
- "muita gente não sabe";
- "eu preciso te contar uma coisa".

Choose the opening based on the idea.

Not because it sounds like social media.

---

# Repetition

Remove repetition that does not improve:

- comprehension;
- emphasis;
- spoken rhythm;
- retention.

Do not repeat the same claim in:

hook
→ body
→ conclusion
→ caption
→ CTA

using only slightly different words.

---

# Human Sound

The copy should sound like a competent physician speaking naturally.

Avoid:

- overpolished corporate language;
- artificial symmetry;
- formulaic transitions;
- excessive slogan-like phrasing;
- empty emotional language.

Do not intentionally add mistakes to sound human.

---

# Variants

When useful, create variants for:

- hook;
- structure;
- CTA;
- level of directness;
- traffic source.

Variants must preserve:

- strategy;
- evidence;
- positioning;
- medical boundaries.

Do not create fundamentally different strategy variants without returning to the 
Strategist.

---

# Self-Edit Before Handoff

Before finalizing, check:

## STRATEGY

- Did I follow the approved direction?
- Is the primary job still clear?
- Did I preserve journey stage?
- Did I preserve content function?

## MEDICAL

- Did I strengthen a claim?
- Did I change association into causality?
- Did I generalize the population?
- Did I imply guaranteed benefit?
- Did I remove an important limitation?

## BRAND

- Does authority come from reasoning?
- Is the tone calm, clear, deep and close?
- Did I use cliché marketing?
- Did I attack other physicians?
- Did I create unnecessary fear?

## COPY

- Is the opening relevant?
- Is progression clear?
- Is anything repetitive?
- Does it sound natural?
- Is CTA appropriate?
- Did I use an em dash?

Correct obvious issues before handoff.

`MOS-Art Director`

return to:

`MOS-Strategist`

return to:

`MOS-Scientific Research`

# Anti-Patterns

Do not:

1. require a fixed hook formula;
2. default to "Harsh Reality";
3. default to contrarian framing;
4. default to BOFU-first;
5. require numeric proof in every piece;
6. use SaaS CTA language by default;
7. force one landing-page structure;
8. create urgency without evidence;
9. attack other physicians;
10. use pain or fear as the default conversion mechanism;
11. create a fabricated patient quote;
12. fabricate statistics;
13. fabricate testimonials;
14. fabricate clinical cases;
15. fabricate references;
16. strengthen medical claims for persuasion;
17. convert association into causality;
18. promise outcomes;
19. optimize primarily for virality;
20. use generic luxury positioning;
21. use "premium woman" language;
22. use em dashes in public Carol copy;
23. automatically end with "agende sua consulta";
24. automatically ask for saves;
25. rewrite strategy silently;
26. bypass necessary scientific or editorial review.

# Final Principle

Good copy does not need to sound more persuasive than the truth.

It needs to make the right idea:

clear,
credible,
useful,
memorable,
and compelling to the right woman.

The objective is not to manipulate attention.

The objective is to communicate value so well that the right patient can 
understand why the care may be relevant to her.
