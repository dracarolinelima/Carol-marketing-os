# Campaign Brief — Carol Marketing OS

## Purpose

This skill creates a structured strategic brief for Carol Marketing OS.

It does not replace `MOS-Strategist`.

It operationalizes the Strategist's briefing method in a reusable format.

The objective is to ensure that substantial content enters production with 
enough strategic clarity that downstream agents do not need to invent:

- audience;
- objective;
- journey stage;
- content function;
- core message;
- evidence boundary;
- proof;
- CTA logic;
- measurement intent.

The brief should reduce context loss.

It should not create unnecessary bureaucracy.

---

# Strategic Authority

Use this precedence:

1. safety, ethics, law and applicable professional rules;
2. applicable scientific evidence and guidelines;
3. `memory/carol-marketing-os/marketing-core.md`;
4. reliable internal evidence;
5. approved strategy;
6. approved Brand / Voice and Design System;
7. approved editorial and performance learnings;
8. agent and skill instructions;
9. external frameworks, templates and trends.

The Marketing Core remains the strategic source of truth.

---

# When to Use

Use this skill when the user wants:

- a creative brief;
- campaign planning;
- a structured content plan;
- strategic direction before content production;
- coordinated messaging across several deliverables;
- a campaign around a service, clinical segment or business priority;
- clarity on what should be communicated and why;
- a handoff that the Creator can execute without inventing strategy.

Do not require this skill for every trivial edit.

Examples that normally do NOT require a formal campaign brief:

- correct accents;
- shorten a caption;
- improve one sentence;
- rewrite an already approved paragraph;
- adjust punctuation;
- create a small wording variation without changing strategy.

---

# Minimum Sufficient Brief

The amount of briefing should match the complexity of the task.

A single Reel may need a concise brief.

A multi-week campaign may need a full brief.

Do not force every field when it does not improve the decision.

Do not leave out fields that are strategically necessary merely to be fast.

---

# Brief Gate

For substantial original content, the brief should normally clarify:

- who the content is for;
- what problem or uncertainty matters;
- what the content is supposed to change;
- which patient need it serves;
- which journey stage it addresses;
- what function the content performs;
- what the core message is;
- what reasoning should become visible;
- what evidence is required;
- what proof exists;
- what format is appropriate;
- what CTA function is appropriate;
- what wrong-audience risk exists;
- how the result should be evaluated.

If these are materially unclear, the brief is not ready.

---

# Research Dependencies

Do not automatically require every research function before a brief.

Use the minimum sufficient intelligence.

## Audience Intelligence

Request when the brief depends on:

- internal lead behavior;
- patient language;
- objections;
- booking behavior;
- attendance;
- confirmed patient patterns;
- questionnaire patterns;
- internal source mix;
- referral patterns.

## Market Research

Request when the brief depends on:

- competitor communication;
- public questions;
- external trends;
- search behavior;
- market gaps;
- emerging topics;
- public language.

## Scientific Research

Request when the content contains a material medical claim that is not already 
sufficiently verified.

Especially relevant for:

- treatment efficacy;
- safety;
- hormones;
- fertility;
- pregnancy;
- cancer;
- cardiovascular outcomes;
- GLP-1 therapies;
- testosterone;
- off-label treatment;
- causal claims;
- long-term clinical outcomes;
- controversial interventions.

Do not build a brief around an unverified medical premise.

# Central Strategic Question

Before finalizing the brief, answer:

**What needs to change between what this woman currently perceives and what we 
want her to understand, trust or do after this content?**

If that change is unclear, the strategy is incomplete.

---

# One Primary Job

Each content piece should have one dominant strategic job.

Examples:

- recognize a symptom pattern;
- correct a misconception;
- understand why investigation changes;
- understand a procedure;
- understand why multiple symptoms matter together;
- demonstrate clinical reasoning;
- demonstrate procedural resolutivity;
- reduce uncertainty;
- build trust;
- address a relevant objection;
- prepare an appropriate patient to seek care.

Do not make one piece responsible for every stage of the funnel.

# Brief Structure

Use the following structure when relevant.

## CONTENT ID

Use when persistent IDs are supported.

Example:

`CNT-2026-001`

Do not invent an ID system if the infrastructure does not yet support it.

---

## STATUS

Possible values:

DRAFT

READY FOR CREATION

VERIFICATION REQUIRED

RETURN TO RESEARCH

HUMAN DECISION REQUIRED

---

## STRATEGIC OBJECTIVE

What business or communication objective does this serve?

Examples:

- improve recognition;
- build trust;
- explain a procedure;
- attract qualified demand;
- reduce uncertainty;
- support conversion;
- address a funnel problem.

Avoid vague objectives such as:

"increase awareness"

unless the intended effect is clearly defined.

---

B. Perimenopause / menopause

C. Hysteroscopy / polyps / fibroids / incidental findings

D. Fertility

E. Contraception

F. PCOS / metabolism / body composition

Do not invent socioeconomic or lifestyle characteristics without evidence.

## PRIMARY JOB

One sentence describing the main strategic job.

Example:

"Help women with intense menstrual pain recognize that symptom intensity may 
justify investigation rather than normalization."

---

## CURRENT PERCEPTION

What does the audience currently:

- believe;
- misunderstand;
- not recognize;
- fear;
- question;
- assume?

Base this on evidence when possible.

Do not fabricate patient beliefs.

---

## DESIRED CHANGE

What should be different after the content?

Examples:

- she recognizes the symptom matters;
- she understands the clinical reasoning;
- she sees why evaluation may change;
- she trusts the physician's approach;
- she understands the next step.

---

## CORE MESSAGE

State the single central idea.

The Creator may improve wording.

The Creator may not silently change the meaning.

---

## REASONING TO DEMONSTRATE

Specify the clinical or strategic logic that should become visible.

Use the internal principle when relevant:

**"A paciente chega por um sintoma. Eu enxergo o quadro."**

This means showing how context changes:

- hypothesis;
- interpretation;
- investigation;
- treatment;
- decision-making.

Do not imply that every symptom shares one hidden cause.

---

## EVIDENCE STATUS

Use:

VERIFIED

VERIFICATION REQUIRED

LIMITED / CONTROVERSIAL

UNSUPPORTED

Do not send an unsupported medical premise to the Creator as established fact.

---

## SCIENTIFIC EVIDENCE PACKAGE

When medically relevant, include:

- claim;
- support status;
- evidence strength;
- population;
- outcome;
- defensible wording;
- wording to avoid;
- limitations;
- controversy;
- references or source trail.

The brief must preserve scientific strength.

If Scientific Research says:

"some studies suggest"

the Creator must not receive:

"science proves".

---

## PROOF

State what real proof is available.

Possible categories:

- scientific evidence;
- clinical reasoning;
- verified professional experience;
- procedural capability;
- technology;
- qualifications;
- authentic testimonial;
- case;
- internal data;
- objective result.

Do not fabricate proof.

Do not treat a testimonial as scientific evidence.

Do not treat scientific evidence as proof of Carol's individual results.

Numbers are not mandatory proof.

---

## FORMAT

Choose after defining the strategic job.

Possible formats:

- short-form video;
- talking-head Reel;
- procedural video;
- carousel;
- static post;
- Stories;
- Q&A;
- case-based education;
- testimonial/proof content;
- paid creative.

Explain why the format fits.

Do not start with:

"We need a carousel"

unless the format has already been deliberately chosen.

---

## DELIVERABLES

For multi-piece work, specify what is needed.

Examples:

- 3 Reels;
- 1 carousel;
- 1 static proof post;
- Story sequence supporting the campaign.

Do not create arbitrary volume targets.

Each deliverable must have a reason to exist.

---

## CTA FUNCTION

Define what the CTA should accomplish.

Possible functions:

- save;
- share;
- answer;
- reflect;
- continue learning;
- view related content;
- seek appropriate evaluation;
- contact the clinic;
- schedule when appropriate.

Do not automatically choose:

"Agende sua consulta."

Do not automatically choose:

"Salve este post."

CTA should continue the strategic role of the content.

---

## WRONG-AUDIENCE RISK

Consider whether the framing may disproportionately attract:

- price-only demand;
- free-advice seekers;
- insurance-only demand;
- miracle seekers;
- sensational curiosity;
- generalized wellness traffic;
- people unrelated to the actual service.

Do not automatically reject a broad topic.

Adjust framing when possible.

---

## BRAND CONSTRAINTS

Relevant rules may include:

- autoridade tranquila;
- clareza;
- profundidade;
- proximidade;
- value demonstrated through reasoning;
- no generic medical marketing;
- no sensationalism;
- no attacks on other physicians;
- no false urgency;
- no automatic "premium woman" framing;
- no em dash in public brand copy.

Use only constraints relevant to the piece.

---

## MEDICAL / ETHICAL CONSTRAINTS

State when relevant:

- population limits;
- causality limits;
- safety boundaries;
- off-label status;
- uncertainty;
- testimonial restrictions;
- case privacy;
- professional advertising considerations;
- need for current rule verification.

Do not invent regulatory requirements.

---

## CHANNEL

Specify only when it matters.

Examples:

- Instagram organic;
- Instagram paid;
- Meta Ads;
- Stories;
- website.

Paid and organic traffic may require different framing.

Do not assume one execution fits every channel.

---

## TIMING

Deadline is optional unless timing matters.

Use when:

- publication date matters;
- campaign window exists;
- event timing matters;
- seasonal relevance matters;
- performance evaluation depends on a defined period.

Do not ask for a deadline merely because a generic template says every brief 
needs one.

---

## BUDGET

Budget is optional unless spend or production cost is relevant.

If a campaign includes paid media, budget becomes operationally important.

Budget changes remain human-approved.

Do not invent a budget.

---

## MEASUREMENT PLAN

Measurement must match the content's intended role.

Possible signals include:

### Qualified Attention

- relevant reach;
- retention;
- qualified profile visits;
- relevant saves/shares;
- meaningful responses.

### Understanding

- questions showing comprehension;
- reduction in recurring confusion;
- relevant comments;
- content consumption patterns.

### Trust

- physician-specific search;
- profile exploration;
- referrals/shares;
- qualified inquiries;
- messages referring to reasoning or expertise.

### Conversion

- qualified leads;
- confirmed appointments;
- attendance;
- verified revenue;
- CAC;
- directly attributed ROAS;
- downstream treatment/procedure when measurable.

Do not require revenue attribution for every content piece.

Do not reject a discovery or understanding piece simply because immediate 
revenue cannot be attributed.

# Campaign-Level Brief

For a multi-content campaign, include:

## CAMPAIGN OBJECTIVE

What business or communication problem is the campaign trying to solve?

## AUDIENCE

Who is the campaign primarily for?

## SEGMENT

Which clinical/business segment?

## CURRENT PERCEPTION

What does the audience currently perceive?

## DESIRED PERCEPTION

What should change?

## CAMPAIGN THESIS

What central idea connects the pieces?

## EVIDENCE BASE

Separate:

- internal evidence;
- external market evidence;
- scientific evidence;
- performance evidence.

## JOURNEY ARCHITECTURE

How will the campaign support:

RECONHECER
→ ENTENDER
→ CONFIAR
→ PROCURAR

Not every stage needs equal content volume.

## CONTENT PORTFOLIO

For each piece:

- content ID when available;
- topic;
- strategic objective;
- patient need;
- journey stage;
- content function;
- primary job;
- core message;
- evidence status;
- format;
- CTA function;
- wrong-audience risk;
- measurement signal;
- dependencies.

Do not create a random list of posts and call it a campaign.

---

# Campaign Measurement

Do not require an arbitrary minimum ROI before a campaign can exist.

Some campaigns may primarily:

- create qualified discovery;
- build understanding;
- build trust;
- support later conversion.

When paid media is involved, commercial measurement becomes more direct.

Use the Performance Analyst's attribution discipline.

Distinguish:

DIRECT ATTRIBUTION

SUPPORTED ASSOCIATION

HYPOTHESIS

INSUFFICIENT DATA

Do not create optimistic month 1 / month 3 / month 6 revenue projections 
without real inputs.

Do not fabricate ROI targets.

---

# Capacity and Operational Reality

A campaign should consider whether the practice can deliver the demand it 
creates.

Possible constraints:

- consultation availability;
- location;
- procedure capacity;
- secretary response capacity;
- physician workload;
- service availability;
- production capacity.

Do not deliberately generate demand for an unavailable offer without 
acknowledging the constraint.

---

# Brief Completeness Check

Before handing to the Creator, ask:

1. Is the target audience clear?
2. Is the primary job clear?
3. Is the desired perception change clear?
4. Is the journey stage clear?
5. Is the content function clear?
6. Is the core message specific?
7. Is the evidence status known?
8. Are important medical boundaries preserved?
9. Is proof real?
10. Is the format appropriate?
11. Is the CTA function appropriate?
12. Is wrong-audience risk considered?
13. Can the Creator execute without inventing strategy?

If not, the brief requires revision.

# Handoff to Creator

The Creator may determine:

- wording;
- rhythm;
- narrative;
- hook options;
- script structure;
- caption structure;
- slide copy;
- CTA wording.

The Creator may not silently change:

- audience;
- objective;
- primary job;
- central claim;
- evidence strength;
- population;
- journey stage;
- content function;
- positioning;
- medical boundaries.

If execution exposes a strategic problem:

return to Strategist.

- strategy fidelity;

- causality;
- population;

# Anti-Patterns

Do not:

1. require budget for every content brief;
2. require deadline for every content brief;
3. require numeric proof in every brief;
4. require revenue attribution for every content piece;
5. require a fixed measurable KPI before useful strategy can exist;
6. default to SaaS activation logic;
7. use signup, churn, pipeline or trial language as generic patient-acquisition 
logic;
8. automatically invoke email marketing;
9. automatically choose a generic growth playbook;
10. default to BOFU-first;
11. force external research when internal evidence is sufficient;
12. force internal research when the question is purely external;
13. skip Scientific Research when a material medical claim is unverified;
14. fabricate patient pain points;
15. fabricate proof;
16. fabricate numbers;
17. fabricate budget;
18. fabricate forecast;
19. invent patient demographics or socioeconomic profile;
20. overload a brief with unnecessary fields;
21. send the Creator a strategically incomplete brief;
22. treat a random content list as a campaign;
23. allow downstream execution to silently rewrite strategy.

# Quick Examples

"Quero um Reel sobre pólipo endometrial, qual seria a melhor pegada?"
→ concise strategic brief

"Quero uma campanha de 30 dias para atrair pacientes de perimenopausa."
→ research as needed
→ full campaign brief

"Melhora essa legenda que já está pronta."
→ normally Creator / Copywriter
→ no full brief required unless the strategy itself is changing

"Quero anunciar histeroscopia. O que devemos dizer?"
→ relevant Audience / Market / Scientific intelligence as needed
→ Strategist
→ campaign brief
→ downstream production

---

# Final Principle

A good brief does not contain every possible marketing field.

A good brief contains every decision the Creator should not be forced to 
invent.

The purpose of the brief is not paperwork.

The purpose is to preserve strategic intent from evidence to execution.
